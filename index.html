The deep end

Good luck hearing the lyrics. These are the heaviest things on the page, and they're not for having on in the background while you do your homework.

Meshuggah: this is an "experimental metal" band from Sweden. You really need headphones to get the nuance in the 8-string guitars.

  1. MeshuggahThirty-second introduction—
  2. MeshuggahBleed2008
  3. Author & PunisherNihil Strength—

    This is literally a one-man industrial doom-metal. He made all the equipment himself. Was a mechanical engineer, decided to go make doom-metal music.

    Short documentary about him

Pieces of music, not songs

Progressive and technical metal. These bands write in odd time signatures and build things slowly over eight or nine minutes. Not background music: they want your whole attention, and they're worth it.

On the last three: these guys make more "pieces of music" rather than "songs". If you remember the binocular CD album that I had, where you could see 3D art, this is them. They have so many good songs, here are my favourites.

  1. GojiraThe Art of Dying2021

    You may remember me playing this at one point in the car.

  2. GojiraAnother World2021

    This is actually a French band, despite being called Gojira.

  3. ToolRight in Two2006

    I think you'll appreciate the themes of this and the next one.

  4. ToolVicarious2006

    I think you'll appreciate the themes of this.

  5. ToolSchism2001

    Fun fact: the opening sound is a slow motion recording of the lead singer's cat being squeezed briefly.

The radio years

Softer edges, bigger choruses, written to be sung by a stadium. No apologies for any of these.

  1. FightstarDeath Car2009

    This helped me get through my anger at being broken into.

  2. Thirty Seconds to MarsThe Kill2005

    This is Jared Leto's (the Joker) band. Listened to this a lot in early 2025.

  3. NickelbackSaving Me2005
  4. MuseTime Is Running Out2003
  5. SeetherCareless Whisper—

    A cover of the George Michael song, played completely straight and very heavy, which turns out to be exactly what it needed.
