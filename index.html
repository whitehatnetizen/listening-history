Forty years of loud music

1984 – 2025 · 52 songs · 10 sections

This is more or less everything I have listened to, arranged by when the music was made rather than when I found it. Some of it I have been playing since I was fifteen. Some of it I found this year.

You do not have to go in order, and you do not have to like any of it. It gets heavier as it goes down the page, and the last few sections are genuinely hard work. That is the point of them.

Where I say to use headphones, use headphones.

1984–1996

Where it starts

The heavy end of the 1980s and early 90s. Everything further down this page grew out of these four songs, whether the bands admit it or not.

  1. MetallicaNothing Else Matters1991
  2. MetallicaFor Whom the Bell Tolls1984

    The original came out in 1984. This link is the remixed version made for the film Spawn in 1997, which is the one I prefer.

  3. PanteraCowboys from Hell1990
  4. SepulturaRoots Bloody Roots1996

    Brazilian. They started putting Amazonian percussion into metal years before anyone else thought it was a good idea.

1994–1997

Grunge and alternative

What was on the radio when I was at school. Quiet verse, enormous chorus, and a singer who sounds like they mean it.

  1. LiveLightning Crashes1994
  2. BushGlycerine1994
  3. BushSwallowed1996
  4. Smashing PumpkinsBullet with Butterfly Wings1995

    I played drums for this song at a high-school talent night in my last year. It is harder than it sounds.

  5. SilverchairFreak1997

    Australian, from Newcastle in New South Wales. They were fifteen when they recorded their first album, which was extremely annoying for everyone else who was fifteen at the time.

  6. RichardaKeep You Always—

1996–2004

Industrial and shock rock

Machinery noises, theatre, and bands who worked out that being frightening on television was a career. Half of this was designed to upset people's parents, which by now includes me.

  1. Marilyn MansonThe Beautiful People1996
  2. Rob ZombieFrom the Matrix soundtrack1999
  3. RammsteinDu Hast1997

    German, and they have never once considered singing in English to make it easier for anybody. Famous for setting large parts of the stage on fire, including occasionally the singer.

  4. RammsteinAmerika2004

1997–2004

The period when weirder was better

Nu-metal. Detuned guitars, hip-hop rhythms, and a race between about forty bands to be the strangest one in the room. This was my late teens and twenties, and it has not aged evenly.

  1. DeftonesMy Own Summer (Shove It)1997
  2. KornFreak on a Leash1998
  3. Korn & SkrillexGet Up!2011

    Same band, thirteen years later, deciding that what metal needed was dubstep. Out of order on this page on purpose, so you can hear what happened to them.

  4. MudvayneDig2000
  5. DisturbedDown with the Sickness2000

    The same band who later did the very serious cover of The Sound of Silence, which is a genuinely difficult fact to hold in your head while this one is playing.

  6. System of a DownChop Suey!2001
  7. System of a DownToxicity2001
  8. Drowning PoolBodies2001

    This one escaped and ended up in every stadium, film trailer and piece of military footage of the next decade. There is also, inexplicably, a version for children.

    The children's version

  9. GodsmackI Stand Alone2002
  10. SlipknotDuality2004

    Nine people, all in masks, all with a number instead of a name. It should be ridiculous and somehow it is not.

2002–2005

Big voices

Symphonic and gothic metal: proper singing on top of the heaviness, often by someone with classical training and an orchestra behind them.

  1. EvanescenceBring Me to Life2003
  2. EvanescenceGoing Under2003
  3. NightwishGhost Love Score2004

    Finnish, operatic, and ten minutes long. If you only listen to one thing on this entire page, make it this one. Give it the full ten minutes and do not check your phone during it.

  4. NightwishPhantom of the Opera2002

    Yes, that Phantom of the Opera. Played straight, at volume.

  5. Cradle of FilthNymphetamine Fix2004

    The woman singing is a guest. The band's actual singer is the one doing the other thing.

  6. FlyleafI'm So Sick2005

2003–2009

The radio years

Softer edges, bigger choruses, written to be sung by a stadium. No apologies for any of these.

  1. MuseTime Is Running Out2003
  2. Thirty Seconds to MarsThe Kill2005

    This is the band belonging to the actor who played the Joker. He is a better singer than that fact suggests. I listened to this a great deal in early 2025.

  3. NickelbackSaving Me2005
  4. SeetherCareless Whisper—

    A cover of the George Michael song, played completely straight and very heavy, which turns out to be exactly what it needed.

  5. FightstarDeath Car2009

    This one got me through a bad stretch after my flat was broken into. Some music is for enjoying and some is for using, and this was the second kind.

2001–2021

Pieces of music, not songs

Progressive and technical metal. These bands write in odd time signatures and build things slowly over eight or nine minutes. Not background music: they want your whole attention and they are worth it.

On the first three: if you remember the album with the binocular case, where you could look through it and see the artwork in 3D, that band is Tool. They have a great many good songs. These are my favourites.

  1. ToolSchism2001

    The sound at the very start is a slow-motion recording of the singer's cat being briefly squeezed. The cat was fine.

  2. ToolRight in Two2006

    Listen to what this one is actually about. I think you will appreciate it.

  3. ToolVicarious2006

    And this one. It is about why people cannot look away from bad news, and it was written before anybody had a phone in their pocket.

  4. GojiraThe Art of Dying2021

    French, despite the name: Gojira is the original Japanese name for Godzilla. You may remember this one from the car.

  5. GojiraAnother World2021

2008 onwards

The deep end

Good luck hearing the lyrics. These are the heaviest things on the page and they are not for having on in the background while you do your homework.

Meshuggah are an experimental metal band from Sweden who write rhythms that sound wrong until suddenly they do not. You really do need headphones to hear what the eight-string guitars are doing.

  1. MeshuggahThirty-second introduction—
  2. MeshuggahBleed2008
  3. Author & PunisherNihil Strength—

    One man, industrial doom metal, and every instrument built by hand. He was a mechanical engineer and decided to go and make this instead, which I think about more often than I expected to.

    Short documentary about how he builds the machines

2013–2025

What I am listening to now

The current end of the list. If any section here is going to work for you, my money is on this one.

  1. Bring Me the HorizonCan You Feel My Heart2013

    From Sheffield, in England. They started out unlistenably heavy and have been getting more melodic every single album since, which is the opposite of what bands normally do.

  2. Bring Me the HorizonDoomed2015

    There is a very good cover of this by MAPHRA

  3. Bring Me the HorizonParasite Eve2020

    Written during the pandemic, and it sounds exactly like that felt.

  4. Five Finger Death PunchWrong Side of Heaven2013

    Ex-military, and they write about veterans and mental health properly rather than as decoration. The video is the point of this one.

  5. Sleep TokenChokehold2023

    Nobody officially knows who is in this band. They wear masks, they do not give interviews, and they have somehow become one of the biggest bands in the world anyway.

  6. Sleep TokenEmergence2025
  7. SkilletInvincible—

    The drummer sings backing vocals at the same time as playing, which is much harder than she makes it look.

  8. InterbeingBlack Halo—

    Niche to the point that almost nobody I know has heard of them. I like this song anyway.

  9. South Arcade2005—

    A new British band deliberately making the exact sound of the year in the title, which is around section four of this page. Everything comes back eventually.
